Discrete emotion labels provide more detail than a single sentiment score. While many posts are labeled **“neutral”** (often reflecting news or analysis), several teams show clearly elevated levels of negative emotions such as **anger, disgust, and sadness**.

Systematic relationships between emotions and performance include:

- **Joy**:
  - Overrepresented in posts about teams that **made the playoffs**.

- **Disgust and fear**:
  - Common for **underperforming teams with losing records**.
- **Neutral tone**:
  - Often appears in analytical or news-style posts.
  - Shows a **weak association with below-average team performance**, possibly reflecting detached or resigned discussion.
- **Anger**:
  - Found in posts about both strong and weak teams.
  - Particularly visible in **high-expectation fanbases**, where anger likely points to frustration with specific game outcomes or managerial decisions rather than fundamental team weakness.

Overall, **emotion profiles distinguish successful from unsuccessful seasons more effectively than raw sentiment alone**. The **mix and intensity of emotions** around a team contain meaningful informational content.

**Core takeaway for the chatbot:**  
When interpreting fan discourse, the model should consider **which emotions dominate** (joy, anger, disgust, fear, etc.) rather than relying purely on a single sentiment score.
