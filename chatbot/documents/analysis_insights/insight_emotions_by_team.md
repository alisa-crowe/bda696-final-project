# Insight: Emotional Tone of Fan Discourse by Team

This heatmap summarizes how frequently each discrete emotion (anger, disgust, fear, joy, neutral, sadness, surprise) appears in fan posts for each MLB team. The patterns reveal meaningful differences in fan responses, expectations, and reactions to team performance.

## Key Findings

### 1. Negative Emotions Cluster Around Underperforming Teams
- Teams with poor records or disappointing seasons show **higher rates of anger, disgust, and sadness**.
- Notable examples include:
  - The **Chicago White Sox** and **New York Yankees**, which exhibit elevated shares of **anger** and **disgust**.
  - The **Oakland Athletics**, **Colorado Rockies**, and **Washington Nationals**, which show increased levels of **sadness** and **fear**.
- These emotional responses reflect frustration, pessimism, and concern among fanbases facing consistent underperformance.

### 2. Joy Appears Primarily in Successful or Surging Teams
- Teams with strong records or positive momentum display **higher proportions of joy**, including:
  - The **Atlanta Braves**, **Los Angeles Dodgers**, **Houston Astros**, and **Tampa Bay Rays**.
- Joy signals excitement around winning streaks, standout performances, and playoff contention.

### 3. Neutral Tone Dominates Analytical or News-Driven Conversations
- Many teams show a large proportion of **neutral** posts.
- This indicates that sports reporting, analysis threads, lineup announcements, and box-score discussions form a major portion of fan discourse.
- Neutral tone is not directly predictive, but it often grows among teams with stagnating performance or disengaged fanbases.

### 4. Emotion Profiles Reveal Fanbase Personality, Not Just Performance
Some fanbases exhibit emotions that diverge from on-field outcomes:

- **New York Yankees** and **Philadelphia Phillies** show higher **anger** despite being competitive teams—reflecting **high expectations** rather than poor results.
- **Seattle Mariners** and **Milwaukee Brewers** display elevated **joy**, even when their performance is not elite—reflecting **optimistic and passionate fan cultures**.
- **Minnesota Twins** and **Boston Red Sox** show predominantly **neutral** or muted emotional responses, suggesting a more **stoic or disengaged** fanbase.

### 5. Surprise Is Rare but Signals Unexpected Events
- The emotion **surprise** appears infrequently overall.
- When present, it likely corresponds to sudden roster moves, unexpected wins, walk-off moments, or shocking losses.

## Interpretation

Emotion labels provide a richer, more textured view of fan reactions than sentiment scores alone. While sentiment captures overall positivity or negativity, **emotions reveal what type of reaction fans are having** — anger, joy, fear, disgust, disappointment, or excitement.

Key patterns:
- **Joy** → aligned with winning, strong run differential, and playoff pushes.  
- **Anger / disgust** → associated with poor records, controversial decisions, or unmet expectations.  
- **Fear / sadness** → reflect concern for struggling teams or long-term decline.  
- **Neutral** → prevalent in analytic, recap, or news-related content.

Emotion distributions therefore mirror not only team performance but also **fan identity, expectations, and narrative context**.

## Implications for the Chatbot

When interpreting team-specific fan discourse or answering questions about fan attitudes:

- Highlight **dominant emotions** for each team, not just sentiment score.
- Explain contradictions when they occur:
  - Example: “Yankees fans show high anger despite strong performance due to elevated expectations.”
- Use emotions to give nuanced insights about team narratives:
  - Joy → excitement and momentum  
  - Disgust / anger → deep frustration or dissatisfaction  
  - Fear → anxiety about future performance  
- Emphasize that **emotional tone can differentiate types of underperformance or success** more effectively than sentiment alone.

This emotional profile data helps the chatbot provide richer, context-aware explanations of fan behavior and team perception.
