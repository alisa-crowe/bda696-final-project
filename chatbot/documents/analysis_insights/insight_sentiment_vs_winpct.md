# Insight: Relationship Between Average Sentiment and Team Win Percentage

This analysis compares each team's average fan sentiment score with its win percentage and examines differences between playoff and non-playoff teams.

## Key Findings

### 1. Sentiment Shows a Positive Relationship with Win Percentage
- Teams with **higher average sentiment scores** tend to have **higher win percentages**.
- Although the relationship is **moderate rather than strong**, it is consistent.
- The correlation indicates that positive fan discourse generally aligns with better on-field results.

### 2. Playoff Teams Exhibit Higher Sentiment and Higher Win% Overall
- **Playoff teams** cluster in the upper-right of the plot:  
  - Higher sentiment  
  - Higher win percentage  
- **Non-playoff teams** cluster lower on both axes:  
  - Lower fan sentiment  
  - Lower win percentage  
- This separation shows that **sentiment helps differentiate playoff-caliber teams from others**, even if it is not a primary predictor.

### 3. Sentiment Is Not a Standalone Predictor
- Some teams with moderate sentiment performed well, and some with higher sentiment underperformed.
- This reflects the fact that sentiment:
  - reacts to performance,
  - captures fan expectations,
  - amplifies wins and losses,
  - but does **not fully explain** success by itself.

### 4. Trendlines Reinforce Directional Predictiveness
Both the playoff and non-playoff groups show **upward-sloping trendlines**, confirming that sentiment rises with better performance regardless of team classification.

### 5. Sentiment Reflects Fan Perception More Than Underlying Quality
- Playoff teams tend to get sustained positive reactions from fans.
- Lower-performing teams show consistently lower sentiment, likely due to frustration, criticism, or lack of excitement.
- Sentiment captures **emotional and narrative context** surrounding a team rather than raw competitive strength.

## Interpretation

Overall, **sentiment is directionally predictive** of team quality but is **not sufficiently strong or precise** to replace statistical performance metrics. Sentiment behaves as a complementary measure that reflects how fans internalize and react to the team’s season.

The division between playoff and non-playoff groups highlights that:
- Successful teams inspire more positive discourse.
- Negative sentiment clusters around teams with losing or disappointing seasons.
- Fan perception amplifies the performance signal but does not create it.
